## What they do

AI systems don't fail like normal software — they can give a confident wrong answer. Evaluation engineers **build the tests that measure AI quality** and keep running them as the system changes.

Companies are moving evaluation from a one-time check at the end to a **continuous** job that happens while the AI is being built.

## How companies are organizing it

| Approach | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Embedded eval teams** | Evaluators sit inside agent-building teams so testing happens during development (described at Google Cloud). |
| **Cross-functional task forces** | Data architects, security officers, and compliance leads review AI tools together. |
| **Hybrid evaluators** | People who combine technical skill with ethics and governance judgment. |
| **AI evaluation leaders** | Connect IT, security, and operations to measure real business impact. |

## Skills

- Writing **test sets** and scoring rubrics
- Diagnosing **why** an agent's reasoning failed
- Monitoring behavior after launch
- Statistics basics — is a change actually better, or just luck?
- Python for automating test runs

## How to break in

1. Pick any AI chatbot and write **50 test questions** with correct answers.
2. Score it, then change the prompt and score again.
3. Publish a short write-up: *what got better, what got worse, and why.*

That single project shows exactly the skill these roles hire for.
